A single sentence that explains the modern internet to a time traveler
who has just stepped out of the year 1800 — using only words and ideas
an 1800 person could grasp.

## The entry

> One small glass box now holds all the books, letters, and songs the world has ever made, and it answers any question in less time than a blink.

**143 characters** — one sentence, one line, within the 200-character limit.

## How it runs

```sh
node main.ts
```

`main.ts` validates the sentence (single line, at most 200 characters,
ends with a period) and writes it to
`$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt` (default `/output/entry.txt`).

## Candidates considered

1. "Every letter ever written, every book ever printed, and every song ever sung now sits in one small glass box, ready to answer any question in the time it takes to blink." (169 chars) — same picture as the winner but wordier; the triple "every … ever …" repeats itself.
2. "Imagine a library larger than every library on earth, with a clerk who finds any page, letter, or picture you ask for before you finish speaking." (145 chars) — a fine image, but it only covers finding things, not holding all of human knowledge in one small object.
3. "All the world's letters, books, songs, and pictures now live together in one tiny glass box that answers any question faster than a blink." (138 chars) — close, but "live together" is softer than "holds", and "pictures" adds little.
4. "A single small box now holds every book, letter, and song mankind has made, and it will show you any of them the moment you ask." (128 chars) — tight, but "small box" is vaguer than "glass box" and it never says how fast the answer comes.
5. "Every map, book, letter, and song ever made now waits inside one small box, and it answers any question before you have finished asking it." (139 chars) — "before you have finished asking it" is clumsy next to the clean "less time than a blink".
6. "Picture a town crier who knows every book, letter, and song ever made, and who can whisper any of them into your ear the instant you ask." (137 chars) — charming, but a crier is a person, not a thing the traveler can picture holding; it hides the object.
7. **"One small glass box now holds all the books, letters, and songs the world has ever made, and it answers any question in less time than a blink." (143 chars) — the winner.** It gives the traveler one concrete object to hold in his head (a small glass box), one impossible-but-true fact (it holds *everything* ever written and sung), and one measure of speed he can feel in his own body (a blink). No word in it was coined after 1800.
8. "All the letters, books, and songs of mankind now live in one small box that will show you any of them, and answer any question, in the blink of an eye." (151 chars) — "in the blink of an eye" is the right image, but the sentence sags in the middle with two "and"s doing the same job.
